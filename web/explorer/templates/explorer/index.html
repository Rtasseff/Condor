      Model 1 projects a constant expected return and dispersion — the simplest honest model. Model 2 resamples 21-day stretches of your actual history, so streaks and crashes look like they really did; its bands are never allowed to come out narrower than model 1's. Shaded bands are market randomness; the dashed outer band adds the uncertainty in the return estimate itself — at multi-year horizons the biggest unknown. A return-assumption control (model 3) comes next.

      Model 2's resampled bands came out narrower than model 1 allows — one decade holds too few multi-year stretches to trust that — so they were widened to model 1's floor.

      These bands assume the future resembles your lookback window in kind, if not in detail — they can't anticipate a change in what your holdings fundamentally are, and the middle line is an estimate, not a promise.
