    Risk & Reward

    Every point on the curve is a portfolio of your assets; the dashed line blends T-bills with the tangent portfolio (past it, you'd be borrowing). Click anywhere near either — the nearest mix lights up below. The key above the chart names each marker. The frontier, in plain words — Learn →

    What am I looking at?
    • Every dot on the blue curve is a portfolio built from your assets. The curve is the set of best possible trade-offs — for any level of risk, the highest reward available. Up and to the left is better.
    • Risk (dispersion) = how widely a year's result tends to swing around the average. Reward = the yearly growth we'd expect if the future behaves like the lookback window.
    • The dashed line blends T-bills (cash) with the tangent portfolio ◆. Left of the diamond = safer mixes with more cash; right of it means borrowing, which most people can't do at that rate.
    • Sharpe ratio = reward earned per unit of risk, after setting aside what T-bills pay for free. Higher is better.
    • Method is how we measure your history, not a view about the economy. Robust statistics use medians, so one wild day can't distort the picture — steadier for volatile stocks. Classic statistics are the textbook mean/covariance approach.

      How these models work
      • Steady rates: projects a constant expected return and dispersion. The simplest honest model.
      • Resampled history: replays 21-day stretches of your actual history, so streaks and crashes look like they really did. Its bands are never allowed to be narrower than steady rates'.
      • The bands: shaded = market randomness; the dashed outer band adds the uncertainty in the return estimate itself — at multi-year horizons, the biggest unknown.
      • Expected return: the whole chart hangs off one number — what the middle line assumes you earn per year. Your own history is a noisy estimate of it, so you can blend that history with a long-run market anchor ({{ market_anchor_pct }}%/yr, held to ±{{ anchor_prior_sd_pct }} points) or with a number of your own. The blend weights each by how well it is known; it never simply replaces your history.
      Advanced — set your expectations (priors)

      A belief about the future, blended with your data by how sure each is — never swapped in. It applies to both models. Return to normal, explained — Learn →

      The resampled bands came out narrower than steady rates allows — one decade holds too few multi-year stretches to trust that — so they were widened to the steady-rates floor.

      These bands assume the future resembles your lookback window in kind, if not in detail — they can't anticipate a change in what your holdings fundamentally are, and the middle line is an estimate, not a promise.
