My account

A ledger-tracked account — pretend money, or a mirror of a real one. Value comes from the last close; putting money in moves the value but never the return. The Build page works fine without this — this is just the tracking.

Total value–holdings + cash at the last close
Net contributions–money you put in, minus taken out
Gain–value minus what you put in
Return (time-weighted)–growth of a dollar — deposits don't count as return

Regular contributions dollar-cost averaging

A fixed amount on a schedule. When it's due you get a reminder on login and a whole-share plan for where to put it — new money goes to whatever has drifted under its setpoint, so contributions do the rebalancing and nothing needs to be sold.

Contribution plan

Buys only — whole shares toward the setpoint; whatever can't buy a whole share (or belongs to the setpoint's cash share) stays in cash. Edit to match what you actually do, then confirm.

How these models work

Same two models as the Build page: steady rates (model 1) or resampled history (model 2, never narrower than model 1). Shaded bands are market randomness; the dashed band adds the uncertainty in the return estimate itself.

The middle line hangs off one number — the return you assume. Your holdings' own history is a noisy estimate of it, so you can blend that with a long-run market anchor ({{ market_anchor_pct }}%/yr, held to ±{{ anchor_prior_sd_pct }} points) or with your own figure. An anchor is a claim about the market, so it applies to the invested part of the account; the cash share keeps earning the T-bill rate.

Model 2's resampled bands came out narrower than model 1 allows — one decade holds too few multi-year stretches to trust that — so they were widened to model 1's floor.

Assumes the future resembles the lookback window in kind and your mix stays as it is today. The middle line is an estimate, not a promise.

How the ledger works

Deposits and withdrawals move cash (contributions, not returns). Buys/sells move shares against cash — price defaults to that day's close. The force types overwrite state when reality and the ledger disagree; the difference is booked as money in/out, not as return.
